How we work

A record outlives the appointment

STIJN MICHIELS ARTS BV was created on 16 September 2026 and is registered with the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises under number 1042.565.797, with its registered office at 32 Hulshout in Brasschaat, province of Antwerp. The registered activity is general medical practice.

The company is a matter of weeks old and there is no sense in dressing that up. What a new practice can be judged on is not accumulated reputation but whether the things that must be in place actually are, and two of those are worth setting out. The first is continuity of care, which Belgian law treats as an obligation rather than a courtesy. A practitioner must ensure that the care they provide continues in their absence, which means arranging for a colleague holding the required competence to take over and making the patient record accessible to them, so that nobody resumes a patient's care without knowing what has happened so far. Participation in organised on-call arrangements belongs to the same duty. The second is the record itself. It has to be retained for at least thirty years after the last contact and may be kept for up to fifty, so a note written at a first consultation may well be read by someone long after everyone involved has forgotten the appointment. That is the argument for writing it properly from the first week rather than from the point at which a practice feels established. Beyond those, a practitioner acts only within their competence and refers beyond it, and patient rights apply in full from day one.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

Your practice is brand new. Why should I register here?

That is a fair question and the honest answer is that the company was registered in September 2026, so there is no track record to point at. What there is instead are the things that are either in place or are not: continuity arrangements for when the practice is unavailable, a patient record kept as the law requires, fees stated before an appointment rather than after, and a willingness to refer rather than to proceed beyond competence. Those are checkable now rather than in five years.

What happens if you are not available?

Care is taken over by a colleague holding the required competence, and your record is made accessible to them. That is not a courtesy arrangement, it is what the legislation on the quality of healthcare practice requires: a practitioner must ensure the continuity of the care they provide, and must make the record reachable so that whoever covers is not working without the necessary information. Participation in organised on-call arrangements is part of the same obligation.

How long is my record kept?

At least thirty years after the last contact, and it may be kept for up to fifty. It will outlive the episode of care that created it by a very long way, which is the main reason a record is worth writing carefully at the time rather than reconstructing later. You have a right of access to it and a right to obtain a copy, and those are rights rather than favours to be requested politely.

What if my problem is outside your competence?

Then you will be told and referred. A practitioner may act only within the limits of their competence and experience and must refer where a matter falls outside them, which is a legal requirement rather than a matter of style. Being referred is not being turned away, and a practice that treats a referral as a failure is a practice that will eventually proceed when it should not.

Why does this page say so little about treatments?

Because the rules on what a doctor may say publicly exclude comparisons with other practitioners, claims of superiority, patient testimonials, success rates and any promise about an outcome. A page containing those things would be both prohibited and, in the way an advertisement needs them, untrue. What belongs on a page like this is practical information about how the practice operates, which is why continuity and the record occupy the space.

Can I find out the fee before I come?

Yes, and you should. Fees applicable to a consultation are stated in advance on request, and the practice's position in relation to the national agreement between physicians and the sickness funds is on the legal page. That status determines whether supplements may be charged above the agreed tariff, and knowing it beforehand removes the only genuinely unpleasant surprise in ordinary primary care.

Fees are stated in advance on request. In an emergency, contact the emergency services.
